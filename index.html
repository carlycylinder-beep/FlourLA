Our story

How it grew.

From flowers brought home from a college job to studios in three cities. A history of what we built, in the founder's own words and years.

2006

Learning the trade

While studying at UCLA, works as a sales associate at Empty Vase, Rita Flora and Mark's Garden. Watches the designers, learns how the businesses run and where the money goes, and gets her first lessons in working with customers. Starts bringing flowers home to experiment.

2008

The first events

Graduates from UCLA in the middle of the housing crisis recession and takes a job at an organ and tissue donor agency. Spends her spare time reading websites and dreaming of a shop like Rita Flora, with food and flowers. A referral from her dad leads to the first event, a Bastille Day party: ten arrangements made on her own, with a local nursery lending cooler space. Next comes a coworker's small wedding for $500, also done alone. Overwhelmed, she keeps going.

2009

Sugar Magnolia Flowers

A roommate urges her not to wait, so the business starts in their El Segundo apartment. She reads about SEO, Entrepreneur and Inc. magazines and the entire SBA website, then files a DBA as a solopreneur for Sugar Magnolia Flowers, named for the Grateful Dead song.

2009 to 2010

$200 and a website

Launches with $200 total. Her brother, Matthew Cylinder, builds the first website. She works evenings as a receptionist at a visual effects studio and builds an e-commerce site for daily flower delivery orders. So many florists nationwide share the Sugar Magnolia name that it has to change. A roommate suggests Flour, since she dreamed of a cafe and flower shop (the bakery never happened). The flourla.com domain turns it into Flour LA, and a designer friend creates the logo.

2010

The first order

Meets SBA SCORE mentors and goes to every networking event she can. The first e-commerce order shocks her, so she googles wholesalers and finds Mayesh near LAX.

2010

An idea for a book

One year in, she imagines The Flower Chef, a do-it-yourself floral book in the spirit of Rachael Ray's 30 minute meals, and studies how Rachael built her brand through cooking demos.

2010

Teaching begins

She contacts local Whole Foods stores and teaches free floral design classes, which lets them promote Flour LA while she practices teaching. Flower mart tours follow, and she rents space at the flower mart to teach classes. A PR acquaintance becomes a big supporter and gets her into Daily Candy, which features her classes.

2010 to 2013

Building the base

Many lower-budget weddings and a few corporate events from referrals, a couple of interns and freelancers, a year in BNI and a women's business group. Money is tight in expensive LA, and she always wants to do large events. She studies other floral design books.

2016

The Flower Chef is published

Grand Central Life & Style publishes The Flower Chef, a #1 new release and bestseller on Amazon.

Today

Three studios, one story so far

Studios in Los Angeles, New York City and Nashville, with installations nationwide. More of the story coming soon.

Questions

Good to know.

Are artificial flowers sustainable?

They can be when they are built to be reused. One installation that returns for many events, seasons or campaigns replaces repeated fresh orders, so fewer cut flowers are grown, shipped and thrown away.

Can you make a custom logo out of flowers?

Yes. Custom floral logos and lettering, on flower walls or as freestanding sculptures, are a core part of our brand activation and convention work.

Do you design flower window displays?

Yes. We design and install artificial and mixed flower window displays for boutiques, brands and retail, and can refresh them for each season or collection.

Do you work in fresh and dried flowers too?

Yes. We design in artificial, fresh and dried flowers, and in mixes of all three. Many installations use a reusable artificial base with fresh, dried or living elements added for each event.

Do you work in Nashville?

Yes. Nashville is home to one of our three studios. Our Nashville focus is custom floral installations, flower walls and sculptures, and large-scale events, including work for The Nashville Edit, Taylor Swift and Tamara Mellon.

Do you travel for events?

Yes. We have studios in Los Angeles, New York City and Nashville and travel throughout the United States, including last-minute events.

Do you teach private floral design classes?

Yes. Private 1:1 design classes start at $1,500. Carly also speaks at conferences and brand events.
